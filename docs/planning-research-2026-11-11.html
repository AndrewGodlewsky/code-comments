Four adjacent skills, and a one-line reminder none of them beat

Issue #50 asked for skill ideas that sit next to the shipped code-comments skill, each researched, built, tested and evaluated on its own. Four were built and run. This page gives the results, what they mean for the project, and what is left for you to decide.

Date: 2026-11-11 Work: Claude agents. Rulings: auditor agents, not a human Graded runs: 660 (plus 84 gate runs) Branch: AndrewGodlewsky/planning-research, not pushed as of 2026-11-11 Spend: not recorded
The short answer
  1. No skill beat its reminder sentence. None of the four skills beat its one-line reminder sentence on graded checks, on either model, in training or on the held-out pair. The fair pooled comparison uses only the runs where all four arms ran: iteration 1 plus held out, 140 runs per arm. Full-mark runs were 132 for the skills and 126 for the reminder (stratified exact p = 0.15). Full-core runs were 137 and 133 (p = 0.28). This means no evidence of a difference. It does not show the two are equal: for a plain two-sided Fisher p < 0.05 on these pooled counts, the reminder would have had to fall to 121/140 or lower.
  2. Guidance beat the bare model, but not on working code. Pooled over the four evals, every guided arm beat the bare model, but only on prose, reply, preservation and restraint checks. The skills and the reminders gained mostly on Haiku; the shipped skill's smaller check gain was mostly on Sonnet. constraint-sweep was the exception: neither the skill nor the reminder gained over bare outside noise there. The reminder tied bare on checks (204/215 each), and the skill's +4 checks and +4 full-mark runs give stratified p = 0.20. On hidden behavioural tests the bare model, the reminder and the skills each passed 120/120 runs. The reminder took about 90% of the skills' margin over bare: 53 of the skills' 59 extra full-mark runs.
  3. The shipped skill, once read, failed hidden tests. This is the one signal about the product that ships today. When read, the shipped code-comments skill was the only arm to fail hidden tests: 111/120 runs, against 120/120 for every other arm. 6 failing runs broke a constraint stated in another file, and 3 built only part of the requested change. Each of the 9 noticed the constraint or error first. They fall in two evals (verify-before-relying, constraint-sweep) and 5 case-model cells, 8 of them on Haiku. All 9 are training runs; on the four held-out pairs the shipped skill passed 40/40 (20/20 on the verify-before-relying and constraint-sweep pairs). The comparison was chosen after reading the runs. The cause, its "make the change if asked and tell the user" conflict rule or its ask-first guidance, is a hypothesis.
  4. Per skill.
    • verify-before-relying: Null against the reminder, a tie at 100%.
    • constraint-sweep: Null against the reminder and the bare model. There was no core headroom: bare passed 35/35 core checks.
    • change-drift-sweep: Null against the reminder and the shipped skill. Its own test, stated before the runs, files it as a plugin enhancement (#21, #48).
    • fold-docs-inline: a clear lead over the shipped skill and the bare model in training (full-mark runs 23/25 against 4/25 and 1/25). On the held-out pair it led on checks (96/100 against 82/100 and 79/100), but at run level only its full-marks lead over bare holds (8/10 against 1/10, stratified exact p = 0.007); on full core it tied bare (8/10 against 7/10). Its held-out lead over the shipped skill (full marks 8/10 against 4/10, stratified p = 0.14; 8/10 against 1/10 before three auditor rulings on shipped runs) and its lead over the reminder (89/100 checks) are Not established. The intent judge placed it outside the 2026-11-09 hygiene narrowing.
  5. Nothing ships. Under the incubator rule, only you can move a skill into a plugin. The three analyses agree that nothing ships and that the offline grader and harness fixes come before any paid run. They differ on what to do with each skill (see Where the analyses disagreed). Any paid run needs your approval and a cost cap. #50's runs rest on your request of 2026-11-11 ("it is totally fine if Opus runs until it hits its usage limit"); no cost cap or estimate was set, and the measured token usage is in the incubator README.
  6. Four things these evals never tested.
    • Whether a skill loads on its own. Every skill arm was told to read its skill.
    • The search steps and the one script, because every 5-16-file fixture could be read whole.
    • ADR 0001's revisit trigger in a large repository.
    • Where a reminder sentence would live. It was only ever appended to the request.
  7. The work was not pushed as of 2026-11-11. It sits in the staging clone on AndrewGodlewsky/planning-research, on top of d37f71d: 16 commits plus the ones that add this page, GLOSSARY.md and the review fixes (count them with git log --oneline d37f71d..HEAD). Nothing from this work was posted to issue #50. To land it: fetch from the staging clone into the real checkout, fast-forward, then push. The guard blocks that for agents here, so you or an unguarded session must do it. #50 stays incomplete until then, and until its review, integration and cleanup gates are met. The steps are in Where the work is.

The ideas

The four ideas: the problem each targets and why each was chosen

The four cover different moments in a change. None is a variant of the shipped skill; each eval's shipped_skill arm tests that claim.

constraint-sweep

C1 · 9/8/7 = 24
Moment
Before an edit, reading context in other files.
Problem
A request that looks local breaks code in another file whose only record of the reliance is a comment there, and the visible tests still pass. ADR 0001 leaves cross-file context to search, and says to revisit if readers "never open or search" the file that holds the fact.
Why chosen
Highest total and on all three top-4 lists. The intent judge called it "the reader half of the user's own mechanism" and the first direct test of ADR 0001's revisit condition.

verify-before-relying

C2 · 8/7/6 = 21
Moment
While writing code that depends on prose.
Problem
New code built on a false units comment, docstring or request claim matches the comment, passes review, and fails at run time. The opposite error is a blanket "code wins" rule, which erases requirements and outside facts. In hygiene iteration 5 a one-line reminder to "fix or delete any comment, docstring or doc that your change makes false" deleted a true vendor quota in 3 of 5 runs.
Why chosen
On every top-4 list, and low effort as instructions only. Narrowed to comments, docstrings and docs, leaving instruction files out.

change-drift-sweep

C3 · 8/7.5/5 = 20.5
Moment
After a change, on prose the change made false.
Problem
A change gives a value a new setting, or renames or moves a flag, key or file, and prose elsewhere still states the old one. It may do so in digits, as a number word, in another unit or as a derived figure. Nothing fails, and the shipped stale-refs check compares no values, so it cannot see this.
Why chosen
On two top-4 lists. It answers intent.md's "nobody knows which documents a change affects". The judges split on C3 against C7: the build followed the intent judge, with C7's diff review as a later mode, while the skeptic had wanted C7 to absorb C3.
