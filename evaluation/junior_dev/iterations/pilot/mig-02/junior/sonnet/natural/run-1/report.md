### Holes
- Q2: What is the "shadow store" physically, and how does the new engine get "the same usage events" as the old engine? Where do usage events come from and how long are they kept? (author: What the shadow store physically is has not been decided. Both engines read the same usage event log, which keeps entries for 35 days (from the brief). The log's technology and how each engine reads it are unknown. Marked open in the document.)
- Q3: Who or what is "on-call" and "the person" who moves forward? How do they trigger a move (a command, a UI, a config edit), and who checks that the gates are actually met before the flag changes? (author: The brief says a person makes forward moves and on-call starts rollbacks, but not the mechanism (command, UI or config edit). I assumed the controller computes gate status and rejects a move whose gates are not met. That is a decision to defend the word 'requires', not a verified fact. Listed as open.)
- Q4: Who measures the "charge failure rate" and how: failed charges divided by attempted charges? What counts as a failure, and what runs the check that triggers the automatic rollback? (author: The brief does not say how the rate is measured. I assumed failed charges over attempted charges in a rolling 10-minute window, checked by the controller. What counts as a failure and any minimum volume are unknown. Listed as open.)
- Q5: The table says 'Who charges' is the new engine in `both`. How do 'workers' and the engines learn which mode they are in from `billing_mode`? What do workers do while the mode flips, and how fast do they see it? (author: How workers and engines read billing_mode (polling, cache, push) and how fast they see a change is unknown. I changed the rollback so it does not depend on speed: the new engine checks the flag and the rollback record before every charge, and step 1 ends only when the new engine confirms it stopped. Both the new-engine behavior and the confirmation are my design additions.)
- Q7: What is the 'ledger', who writes to it, and how does the old engine in step 4 check it? Does the ledger record charges from the new engine in the same format the old engine can read? (author: The brief says the ledger is the system of record for charges, written by the old engine in old_only and shadow, and by the new engine in both and new_only. Whether the old engine can read the new engine's ledger entries in the same format is not known. I assumed it can, keyed by customer and billing period. Listed as open.)
- Q10: What does 'Finance sign-off' look like in the system, and what is an 'unresolved mismatch' in both: how is a mismatch resolved and by whom? (author: Neither the form of Finance sign-off nor the way a mismatch is resolved, or by whom, is specified in the brief. Both are listed as open. The document does say that an unresolved mismatch or missing sign-off blocks the move to new_only.)
- Q11: The doc says the rollback progress record is an assumption awaiting confirmation. Is it confirmed? Also, what happens if a rollback is triggered automatically but the config service itself is unavailable? (author: Not confirmed. The rollback record in the config service is still my assumption and needs your confirmation. If the config service is down during an automatic rollback, the controller cannot record the pause or flip the flag. I do not know what the system should do, so the document now lists it as an open question and a row in the failure table.)
- Q15: How is the 0.1 pct mismatch rate calculated: mismatched customers over customers compared, per night? Is the 5000 invoices count per night, or total over the 7 days? (author: The brief does not define this. I assumed the nightly rate is mismatched invoices over invoices compared that night, each of 7 nights must be under 0.1%, and 5,000 is the total across the 7 nights. I cannot defend per-night versus total from the brief. Now stated in Open questions.)
- Q24: What is the charge key's billing period when a customer is charged for a period that was split across engines? Can one customer have more than one charge per period (for example usage-based top-ups), and would the key then block a legitimate second charge? (author: Decided part: a billing period is charged whole by one engine, whichever is charging when it falls due, because both engines read the same usage events and compute the full period; a period is never split across engines for charging, and the ledger check stops the other engine charging it again. Unknown part: whether a customer can have more than one legitimate charge per period (for example usage top-ups). The brief says never charge twice for a billing period, so I assumed one charge per customer per period; if that is wrong the charge key would block a legitimate second charge. I cannot defend the assumption from the brief, so it is marked in Terms and the open questions.)
